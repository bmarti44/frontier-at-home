# Adversarial falsification review: F14-prefetch-neutral

sol xhigh was tasked to PROVE THE CLAIM FALSE (not merely review it).

---

---FINAL---
VERDICT: FALSIFIED

1. The “46% fewer demand misses” metric is mislabelled. Baseline reports 463.6 + 136.4 = 600 accesses/token; prefetch reports only 463.6 + 74.0 = 537.6. The missing 62.4 are prefetched claims. The implementation increments the real cache-miss counter before ds4_pf_claim, while LOADPROF prints only miss_jobs.size() (ds4-iq2xxs-down-cuda.patch:2731-2736, 2987-2991). Therefore cache misses remain approximately 136.4/token; only synchronous demand-read jobs fall to 74.0.

2. The proposed predictor-sync attribution does not fit the “other” bucket. The extra ds4_gpu_end_commands() executes in the caller’s predictor path (patch:58-100). The LOADPROF timer starts later, inside cuda_stream_selected_cache_begin_load (patch:2558-2567). Thus that sync cannot be directly booked as loader “other.” Assigning all 46.6 ms to 75 syncs would require 0.621 ms/sync and about 2,936 ms over the 63-token window. Yet wall time changes only +53 ms. After accounting for the loader’s 4.9 ms/token improvement, all work outside the loader increased about 362 ms/request, or 5.74 ms/token and at most roughly 0.078 ms per predictor transition. The suggested 46.6-ms predictor-sync hypothesis is about eight times too large.

3. The copy-call hypothesis is numerically plausible but not proven as pure copy bandwidth. The 62.4 inferred claims/token, two complete 9.28-MiB slab movements, and +46.6 ms imply 1,158 MiB/token at 24.3 GiB/s, or 0.747 ms per claimed slab. That matches the six synchronous cudaMemcpy calls located inside the unclassified loader interval (patch:2742-2773). But there are no timers around those calls. “Other” also captures their synchronization and pipeline-drain effects, cache/map work, and the final blocking selected-ID upload. The evidence supports “blocking consume path,” not the narrower assertion “46.6 ms of byte-copy cost.”

4. Copies are not the whole explanation for net-neutral wall time. Loader time improves from 242.8 to 237.9 ms/token, worth 308.7 ms across 63 tokens, while request wall time worsens by 53 ms. Therefore approximately 361.7 ms/request of additional cost appears outside LOADPROF. Predictor GEMV/select/readback, its device synchronization, speculative-reader contention, or run noise consumed the remaining loader gain. The ledger’s statement that wall neutrality is caused by the +46.6-ms consume bucket omits this measured residual.

5. The missing 0.6 ms per eliminated read is a major confound. Baseline fetch averages 196.1/136.4 = 1.438 ms/job. Residual prefetch demand jobs average 145.2/74.0 = 1.962 ms/job, 36% slower. Linear scaling predicts 89.7 ms saved by eliminating 62.4 jobs, but only 50.9 ms is observed: a 38.8-ms shortfall, or 0.622 ms/eliminated job. The fetch timer covers a six-thread pool’s makespan, including pread, host-to-device cudaMemcpy, thread startup, and join—not isolated disk time (patch:2853-2903). Parallel batches are non-additive, and six speculative readers compete with six demand readers. Calling the 50.9-ms delta “disk saving” is therefore false.

6. Page-cache warming is possible but not demonstrated. Both paths attempt O_DIRECT, while both have fallback paths (patch:2046-2064 and 2882-2888). The three-line evidence file records neither whether O_DIRECT succeeded nor physical NVMe bytes, latency, or queue depth. More importantly, a served prefetch does not eliminate a disk read globally: the worker already read that slab earlier; it merely prevents a later demand read. The arithmetic gap is better explained by parallel-makespan effects and contention than by assuming the saved reads were cheap page-cache hits.

7. The actual worker performs much worse than the shadow bandwidth forecast. Its counters show 25,431 completed fills, 12,704 served, and 12,727 expired. Because served + expired exactly equals filled, only 49.95% of completed reads were timely useful, with essentially one wasted completed read per useful one. That is materially worse than the cited 67.7% shadow precision and “1.02x” speculative bandwidth. These cumulative counters are not scoped to the 63-token measured window, so they cannot validate “~65 slabs/token,” but they do prove substantial wasted I/O capable of causing the residual-demand slowdown.

8. The proposed “~6 us HIT” is an enqueue-time illusion. The hit timer surrounds three cudaMemcpyAsync calls and stops immediately after enqueue (patch:2706-2728). Its 2.7 ms/463.6 = 5.82 us/hit does not include transfer completion. Those hits represent about 4.20 GiB/token; treating 2.7 ms as completed transfer time implies 1,556 GiB/s, exposing the measurement error. Completion is paid later through stream ordering or a blocking operation. Reserve-fill-publish cannot legitimately claim that a slab has been consumed for 6 us.

9. Reserve-fill-publish removes neither all copies nor all contention. The worker currently performs aligned pread into private staging followed by memcpy into its destination (patch:2046-2061). Filling the arena directly merely changes that destination: stage-to-arena still happens, and demand still requires arena-to-device staging. It can eliminate the device-to-arena round trip and replace sequential blocking copies with asynchronous hit-style copies, but it moves part of the cost out of “other” rather than eliminating the entire consume cost.

10. The proposed reservation policy contradicts the cited review. The quoted reserve/fill/publish-last remark concerned preventing publication before bytes were complete (sol-prefetch-review.txt:50-56). The same review explicitly requires “Never admit at hint or READY completion” and permits speculative arena use only from an empty slot or by evicting another speculative entry (lines 68-76). Reserving ordinary arena slots at prediction time can evict demand-cached experts for the 50% of completed predictions that expire. A dedicated speculative partition and explicit RESERVED/FILLING/READY ownership would be required; this is not an already-identified drop-in fix.

11. The evidence cannot support a strong causal claim. prefetch-ab.txt contains only two aggregate lines and one cumulative counter line. It omits raw per-layer records, wall timings, O_DIRECT status, cache-state digests, predictor-only and consume-only ablations, and repetitions. The harness uses one fixed prompt, two warmups, then one measured request (glm_profile_probe.sh:45-56), not cold-reset randomized ABBA trials. Consequently “within noise,” “+46.6 ms is copies,” and “the fix will convert −51 ms into wall gain” are assertions, not results established by the cited evidence.
