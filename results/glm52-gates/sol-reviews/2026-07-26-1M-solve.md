Blunt answer: **no configuration available in the current engine reaches 1M.** Several proposed layouts fit on paper, but all require unimplemented storage/offload changes, and every in-RAM layout that respects the box’s observed residency cuts the expert cache to roughly 31–43 GiB. Even if one passes fidelity, it will be nowhere near DSV4-class performance.

## 1. Joint memory optimization

The simplified ledger is misleading:

\[
119.7-C_{\rm context}-E_{\rm experts}-6
\]

It says FP4/F32-indexer permits 62.0–65.5 GiB of experts, so 60 GiB fits.

The observed ledger includes memory already occupied outside the context and expert arena. The 32K profile had approximately 10.2 GiB of context and 11.7 GiB available, so:

\[
M_{\rm other}=119.7-67-10.2-11.7=30.8\text{ GiB}
\]

Preserving 6 GiB free gives:

\[
E_{\max}=119.7-30.8-C_{\rm 1M}-6
\]

That is the ledger I would trust until a target build proves otherwise.

| 1M storage | Indexer | Total context | Max experts, simplified | Max experts, observed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FP4, 1-byte scale/32, F16 RoPE | F32 | 48.2 GiB | 65.5 GiB | **34.7 GiB** |
| FP4, F32 scale/32, F16 RoPE | F32 | 51.7 GiB | 62.0 GiB | **31.2 GiB** |
| FP4, 1-byte scale/32, F16 RoPE | F16 | 43.2 GiB | 70.5 GiB | **39.7 GiB** |
| FP4, F32 scale/32, F16 RoPE | F16 | 46.7 GiB | 67.0 GiB | **36.2 GiB** |
| FP4, 1-byte scale/32, F16 RoPE | FP8 + F32 scale/64 | 40.85 GiB | 72.9 GiB | **42.1 GiB** |
| FP4, 1-byte scale/32, F16 RoPE | FP4 + scales | 39.5–39.8 GiB | 73.9–74.2 GiB | **43.1–43.4 GiB** |
| Strict FP8 cKV + F32 RoPE | F32 | 77.3 GiB | 36.4 GiB | **5.6 GiB** |

Specific observed-ledger configurations that arithmetically fit are therefore:

- FP4/E8-scale cKV, F16 RoPE, F32 indexer, 34 GiB experts:

  \[
  30.8+48.2+34+6=119.0\text{ GiB}
  \]

- FP4/E8-scale cKV, F16 RoPE, F16 indexer, 39 GiB experts:

  \[
  30.8+43.2+39+6=119.0\text{ GiB}
  \]

- FP4/F32-scale cKV, F16 RoPE, F16 indexer, 36 GiB experts:

  \[
  30.8+46.7+36+6=119.5\text{ GiB}
  \]

The allocations really are separate in the source: latent, RoPE, and indexer arrays are independently allocated [here](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:39197). Therefore F16/FP8 indexer storage is mechanically separable. It requires splitting the current shared compact-cache dtype flag, not redesigning the graph.

But quantizing the indexer is semantically dangerous. The scorer evaluates all rows and then selects top-2048 [here](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:46030). If quantization changes the 2048th/2049th ordering:

- one cKV row is removed and another inserted;
- that selection is reused by subsequent non-indexer layers, commonly four layers;
- attention changes discontinuously;
- later hidden states and later indexer selections can diverge.

A margin flip might be harmless when the swapped rows are equivalent, or material when the removed row carries unique information. There is no defensible generic tolerance. Measure boundary margins, recall/Jaccard@2048, swapped-row attention mass, and ultimately paired NLL.

### Expert-cache cost

Your 67→60 estimate is correct conditional on a 3–4 pp hit loss:

\[
5.8\times(0.03\text{–}0.04)=0.174\text{–}0.232\text{ GB/token}
\]

At 10.7 GB/s, that adds 16–22 ms/token. Starting from 429 ms/token:

\[
2.33\rightarrow2.25\text{–}2.22\text{ tok/s}
\]

That is 3.6–4.8% slower.

The hit-loss premise is not proven:

- Belady 67→60: 88.0→86.2%, only 1.8 pp.
- Interpolated same-trace LRU at 67 versus 60: approximately 73.6→70.9%, 2.7 pp.
- Current 77.6% is a different policy/profile measurement.

More importantly, **60 GiB does not fit the observed ledger**. At 40 GiB, measured LRU is 59.7%; relative to the current 77.6%, that adds 1.04 GB/token or about 97 ms/token, reducing 2.33 to approximately **1.90 tok/s before adding the 1M indexer cost**. Actual 31–40 GiB current-policy hit rates are unverifiable; the supplied curve stops at 40 GiB.

## 2. Is 1M useful?

Not as DSV4 parity.

At 1M, per generated token:

- F32 indexer keys: **10.75 GB**
- logical expert weights requested: **5.8 GB**
- expert NVMe misses:

  - 77.6% hit: 1.30 GB
  - assumed 74.2% hit: 1.50 GB
  - 40 GiB LRU, 59.7% hit: 2.34 GB

- FP4 attention cKV for selected rows: only 0.064–0.072 GB
- score output/top-k adds further traffic and work

The 10.75 GB number is only a byte floor. The scorer also performs 32 weighted head dot products per row and top-k over one million scores.

A useful throughput equation is:

\[
t_{\rm 1M}=0.429+
\frac{5.8(0.776-h_{\rm new})}{10.7}
+T_{\rm indexer}(1M)
+T_{\rm codec/offload}
\]

At the Spark’s 273 GB/s peak, the F32 indexer byte floor is 39 ms/token; F16 is 20 ms. Therefore:

| Scenario | Pre-indexer TPOT | Ideal indexer | Optimistic result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 60 GiB, assumed 74.2% hit, F32 index | 447 ms | 39 ms | **2.06 tok/s** |
| 60 GiB, assumed 74.2% hit, F16 index | 447 ms | 20 ms | **2.14 tok/s** |
| 40 GiB LRU, 59.7% hit, F32 index | 526 ms | 39 ms | **1.77 tok/s** |
| 40 GiB LRU, 59.7% hit, F16 index | 526 ms | 20 ms | **1.83 tok/s** |

Those are optimistic bandwidth ceilings, not forecasts. With the 40 GiB case and effective F32 scorer bandwidth of:

- 100 GB/s: **1.58 tok/s**
- 50 GB/s: **1.35 tok/s**
- 25 GB/s: **1.05 tok/s**
- 7 GB/s: approximately **0.49 tok/s**

So: **0.5 tok/s is possible if the real score+top-k chain achieves only about 7 GB/s effective. It is not established.** Nor is 1.8 tok/s established. “Actual 1M tok/s” is presently unverifiable.

The cheapest way to answer it is a standalone scorer benchmark: allocate one 1M×128 indexer tensor, invoke the existing score-one plus top-k kernels 21 times, and test F32 and F16. Reusing one 512 MB tensor is sufficient; no 1M context allocation or FP4 work is needed.

Cold construction is worse. At 23–32 prefill tok/s, naïve linear extrapolation is **8.7–12.1 hours for 1M tokens**. The indexer’s cumulative work is actually quadratic during prefill:

\[
21\times128\times4\times\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
\]

At \(N=1M\), that is about **5.38 PB of F32 indexer-key reads**, with an ideal 273 GB/s floor of roughly **5.5 hours just for indexer bytes**. A 1M mode is therefore primarily useful for restored/prebuilt contexts, not interactive cold ingest.

Do not write a packed FP4 kernel first. Add one env-gated F32 fake-quant/capture path.

Use layers:

This covers early/middle/late layers, six full-indexer layers, and the final non-indexer layer. Full-indexer placement is defined [here](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:34421).

### Hooks

In `glm_graph_forward_indexed_tokens`:

1. Immediately after `ds4_gpu_glm_store_compact_kv_tensor` [here](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:44418):

   - dump the actual 512-wide rows from `layer_kv_lora_cache[il]`;
   - optionally also dump `batch_kv_norm` to distinguish pre-store from actual stored semantics;
   - leave `layer_k_rope_cache` and `layer_indexer_key_cache` untouched.

2. Immediately after `batch_qk_low` is computed [here](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:44639):

   - dump 128 stratified token positions per layer;
   - each position has 64×512 latent queries, giving 8,192 real query vectors per layer;
   - dump `last_indexer_selected` for those positions.

3. Optionally dump sampled `batch_heads` or `batch_attn_lora` after attention to measure attention-output error directly.

In `glm_graph_forward_token`, apply the same post-store hook after both the fused store at line 45817 and normal store [here](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:45963), and capture `qk_low` after line 46076. Hooking post-store avoids missing the fused path.

Dump a binary header containing engine/model hash, layer, prompt ID, absolute position, dtype, candidate codec, block size, and fixed random-sign seed.

### Dump size

- 8 layers × 8192 × 512 × F32 cKV: **128 MiB**
- pre-store cKV too: another **128 MiB**
- 8192 F32 query vectors/layer: **128 MiB**
- 128 top-2048 selections/layer: **8 MiB**
- sampled attention heads: approximately **64 MiB/run**

Minimal useful capture: about **264 MiB**. Pre+post cache plus outputs: about **456 MiB**.

### Analysis

For each real post-store row \(c\), test:

- plain E2M1/group-32;
- fixed random-sign BDR-128 → E2M1/group-32 → inverse BDR;
- 1-byte exponent and F32-scale variants.

Compute:

- per-channel RMS, max, kurtosis;
- per-block `amax/RMS`;
- covariance spectrum and stable outlier channels;
- ordinary reconstruction error;
- query covariance

  \[
  M_l=\mathbb{E}_{t,h}[q_{l,t,h}q_{l,t,h}^{T}]
  \]

- query-weighted score error

  \[
  \frac{\delta c^T M_l\delta c}{320}
  \]

  because the attention score scale is \(1/\sqrt{256+64}=1/\sqrt{320}\);

- sampled attention-output error;
- later-layer selection Jaccard/margin crossings.

That capture can genuinely kill a codec, but it cannot clear fidelity. Clearance requires the same candidate as an F32 round-trip applied to all 78 latent caches, leaving RoPE and indexer unchanged, followed by:

- paired teacher-forced NLL;
- mean dNLL ≤ 0.01 nat/token;
- top-1 loss ≤ 0.5 pp;
- several >2048 and 8K–32K scored suffixes so the sparse-index path is exercised.

If that fails, stop FP4. If it passes, FP4 attention-cKV fidelity is cleared; packed storage and performance remain separate engineering gates.

## 4. Cold cKV offload

This is the strongest alternative because it can preserve attention cKV exactly.

Keep the all-row indexer in RAM. After each full-indexer top-k:

1. fetch the 2048 selected cKV rows from NVMe;
2. stage them into a contiguous 2048-row buffer;
3. run attention against staged row IDs 0…2047;
4. reuse the same selection/staging organization for subsequent non-indexer layers.

Store rows grouped by the 21 indexer-selection epochs so a selected row contains all one-to-four layers that reuse that selection.

Memory:

- exact F32 attention cKV on disk: **167.4 GiB/context**
- in-RAM non-cKV context, F32 indexer: **19.14 GiB**
- max observed-ledger experts: **63.76 GiB**
- with F16 indexer: 14.14 GiB and **68.76 GiB** max experts

Thus two viable paper configurations are:

- exact F32 cKV offloaded, F32 indexer in RAM, 63 GiB experts;
- exact F32 cKV offloaded, F16 indexer in RAM, full 67 GiB experts, conditional on the indexer fidelity gate.

NVMe decode payload:

- useful F32 cKV bytes: **0.368 GB/token**
- with 4 KiB alignment and 21 grouped reads: approximately **0.495 GB/token**
- operations: about **43,000 random reads/token**

The throughput floor is 46 ms/token at 10.7 GB/s, but IOPS, synchronization, and sharing the drive with expert misses may dominate. With 67 GiB experts at 77.6% hit, total NVMe payload is approximately:

\[
1.30+0.495=1.80\text{ GB/token}
\]

The ideal cKV-offload/F16-indexer result is roughly:

\[
0.429+0.020+0.046=0.495\text{ s/token}=2.02\text{ tok/s}
\]

That is worth prototyping, but only after a trace-replay gather benchmark proves the 43K-I/O pattern.

Do not offload the indexer. F32 indexer + cKV + current expert misses would be approximately:

\[
10.75+0.495+1.30=12.55\text{ GB/token}
\]

That is 1.17 seconds of NVMe time alone, yielding at best roughly **0.65–0.7 tok/s** after other work. The O(context) indexer read does not automatically kill a RAM-indexer/cold-cKV design; it definitively kills an NVMe-indexer design.

## 5. Ranked plan

1. **Tell the user now that 1M is not supported or DSV4-equivalent.** Highest value, certainty, essentially zero cost. This ranks first.

2. **Run the 1M indexer score+top-k microbenchmark**, F32 and F16, plus trace replay at 60/40/approximately 34 GiB expert arenas. One afternoon. This determines whether the mode is 1.8, 1.0, or 0.5 tok/s before expensive codec work.

3. **Add the capture plus F32 fake-quant arm.** This is the cheapest experiment that can genuinely kill or clear FP4 attention-cKV fidelity. Do not build packing first.

4. **If FP4 fails, benchmark exact F32 cKV NVMe gathers** using real selected-row traces and the grouped-on-disk layout. Kill it unless sustained shared-drive performance remains acceptable with expert reads active.

5. **Only then implement packed FP4 or independent indexer quantization.** Indexer F16 is the first candidate; FP8/FP4 indexer storage buys more memory but has the sharpest correctness discontinuity.

The best defensible statement is:

- **Today:** advertise 32K, with functional evidence only through 11,648 tokens.
- **Next credible target:** 128K after strict-FP8 integration and fidelity/allocation gating.
- **Memory-only ceiling with the present expert footprint:** roughly 159K, not a supported context claim.
- **1M on this box:** research-only, likely around 1–2 tok/s if everything favorable lands, potentially 0.5 tok/s if the scorer is poor, and hours to build cold. It is not DSV4-class long-context parity.

- 8 layers × 8192 × 512 × F32 cKV: **128 MiB**
- pre-store cKV too: another **128 MiB**
- 8192 F32 query vectors/layer: **128 MiB**
- 128 top-2048 selections/layer: **8 MiB**
- sampled attention heads: approximately **64 MiB/run**

Minimal useful capture: about **264 MiB**. Pre+post cache plus outputs: about **456 MiB**.

### Analysis

For each real post-store row \(c\), test:

- plain E2M1/group-32;
- fixed random-sign BDR-128 → E2M1/group-32 → inverse BDR;
- 1-byte exponent and F32-scale variants.

Compute:

- per-channel RMS, max, kurtosis;
- per-block `amax/RMS`;
- covariance spectrum and stable outlier channels;
- ordinary reconstruction error;
- query covariance

  \[
  M_l=\mathbb{E}_{t,h}[q_{l,t,h}q_{l,t,h}^{T}]
  \]

- query-weighted score error

  \[
  \frac{\delta c^T M_l\delta c}{320}
  \]

  because the attention score scale is \(1/\sqrt{256+64}=1/\sqrt{320}\);

- sampled attention-output error;
- later-layer selection Jaccard/margin crossings.

That capture can genuinely kill a codec, but it cannot clear fidelity. Clearance requires the same candidate as an F32 round-trip applied to all 78 latent caches, leaving RoPE and indexer unchanged, followed by:

- paired teacher-forced NLL;
- mean dNLL ≤ 0.01 nat/token;
- top-1 loss ≤ 0.5 pp;
- several >2048 and 8K–32K scored suffixes so the sparse-index path is exercised.

If that fails, stop FP4. If it passes, FP4 attention-cKV fidelity is cleared; packed storage and performance remain separate engineering gates.

## 4. Cold cKV offload

This is the strongest alternative because it can preserve attention cKV exactly.

Keep the all-row indexer in RAM. After each full-indexer top-k:

1. fetch the 2048 selected cKV rows from NVMe;
2. stage them into a contiguous 2048-row buffer;
3. run attention against staged row IDs 0…2047;
4. reuse the same selection/staging organization for subsequent non-indexer layers.

Store rows grouped by the 21 indexer-selection epochs so a selected row contains all one-to-four layers that reuse that selection.

Memory:

- exact F32 attention cKV on disk: **167.4 GiB/context**
- in-RAM non-cKV context, F32 indexer: **19.14 GiB**
- max observed-ledger experts: **63.76 GiB**
- with F16 indexer: 14.14 GiB and **68.76 GiB** max experts

Thus two viable paper configurations are:

- exact F32 cKV offloaded, F32 indexer in RAM, 63 GiB experts;
- exact F32 cKV offloaded, F16 indexer in RAM, full 67 GiB experts, conditional on the indexer fidelity gate.

NVMe decode payload:

- useful F32 cKV bytes: **0.368 GB/token**
- with 4 KiB alignment and 21 grouped reads: approximately **0.495 GB/token**
- operations: about **43,000 random reads/token**

The throughput floor is 46 ms/token at 10.7 GB/s, but IOPS, synchronization, and sharing the drive with expert misses may dominate. With 67 GiB experts at 77.6% hit, total NVMe payload is approximately:

\[
1.30+0.495=1.80\text{ GB/token}
\]

The ideal cKV-offload/F16-indexer result is roughly:

\[
0.429+0.020+0.046=0.495\text{ s/token}=2.02\text{ tok/s}
\]

That is worth prototyping, but only after a trace-replay gather benchmark proves the 43K-I/O pattern.

Do not offload the indexer. F32 indexer + cKV + current expert misses would be approximately:

\[
10.75+0.495+1.30=12.55\text{ GB/token}
\]

That is 1.17 seconds of NVMe time alone, yielding at best roughly **0.65–0.7 tok/s** after other work. The O(context) indexer read does not automatically kill a RAM-indexer/cold-cKV design; it definitively kills an NVMe-indexer design.

## 5. Ranked plan

1. **Tell the user now that 1M is not supported or DSV4-equivalent.** Highest value, certainty, essentially zero cost. This ranks first.

2. **Run the 1M indexer score+top-k microbenchmark**, F32 and F16, plus trace replay at 60/40/approximately 34 GiB expert arenas. One afternoon. This determines whether the mode is 1.8, 1.0, or 0.5 tok/s before expensive codec work.

3. **Add the capture plus F32 fake-quant arm.** This is the cheapest experiment that can genuinely kill or clear FP4 attention-cKV fidelity. Do not build packing first.

4. **If FP4 fails, benchmark exact F32 cKV NVMe gathers** using real selected-row traces and the grouped-on-disk layout. Kill it unless sustained shared-drive performance remains acceptable with expert reads active.

5. **Only then implement packed FP4 or independent indexer quantization.** Indexer F16 is the first candidate; FP8/FP4 indexer storage buys more memory but has the sharpest correctness discontinuity.

The best defensible statement is:

- **Today:** advertise 32K, with functional evidence only through 11,648 tokens.
- **Next credible target:** 128K after strict-FP8 integration and fidelity/allocation gating.
- **Memory-only ceiling with the present expert footprint:** roughly 159K, not a supported context claim.
- **1M on this box:** research-only, likely around 1–2 tok/s if everything favorable lands, potentially 0.5 tok/s if the scorer is poor, and hours to build cold. It is not DSV4-class long-context parity.
