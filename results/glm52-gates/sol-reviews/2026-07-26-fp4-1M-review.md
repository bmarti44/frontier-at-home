Blunt bottom line: do not claim “1M with no noticeable degradation” as achievable today. It is a plausible research target only after sacrificing a large fraction of the expert cache, but the present fidelity argument does not establish it. With the full 67 GiB expert cache, corrected FP4 accounting does not fit physically.

### (a) T-A inference chain

**Verdict: unsound. The 1.88× MSE reduction does not justify dividing NLL by 1.88.**

For fixed queries, key error first perturbs logits:

`δz_i = qᵀδk_i / √d`

For `p = softmax(z)`:

`KL(p || softmax(z+δz)) = log E_p[e^δz] − E_p[δz] ≈ ½ Var_p(δz)`

The first-order attention-output error is:

`δo ≈ Σ p_i δv_i + Σ p_i δz_i(v_i − o)`

Downstream token loss is then:

`Δℓ = gᵀδo + ½δoᵀHδo + …`

Raw reconstruction energy predicts NLL only under restrictive conditions: small errors, approximately zero bias, stable Jacobians, and error covariance aligned similarly before and after rotation. Deterministic E2M1 error is structured, MLA latent error affects both effective K and V, and DSA top-k introduces a discontinuity. Calling energy scaling “first order” at [T-A line 15](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_fp4_rotation_snr.py:15) is backwards: MSE is already second order.

The `0.0296` anchor is also not transferable. Its arithmetic is roughly correct—`ln(6.45/6.26) ≈ 0.0299`—but the experiment used DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite 16B, 512-token windows, only 7,935 tokens, and accounts for 16 separate K/V heads rather than GLM-5.2’s one 576-wide stored latent. It is evidence that naïve Q4 can hurt, not a calibration constant for this cache. [Original Q4 experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04428)

Measure, in increasing order of authority:

- Query-weighted key error `δkᵀE[qqᵀ]δk`, attention-logit MSE and attention KL.
- Attention-output error, including the effective value path.
- DSA top-k recall and score-margin crossings.
- Ultimately, paired teacher-forced NLL/top-1 with the real FP4 fake-quant path.

SAW-INT4 explicitly uses `δkᵀMδk`, not raw MSE, as its attention-sensitive objective. [SAW-INT4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19157v1)

### (b) Synthetic data

**Verdict: not a defensible stand-in for GLM-5.2 MLA latents. The 2.74 dB result is presently meaningless for the target model.**

The test imposes a fixed set of channels scaled exactly 30× at [lines 69–80](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_fp4_rotation_snr.py:69). GLM’s latent is RMS-normalized immediately before storage at [ds4.c line 45952](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:45952). RMS normalization does not rule out channel anisotropy, but neither does conventional KV-key literature prove that this learned, low-rank latent has stable 30× channel outliers.

Worse, the engine’s 128-wide Hadamard/FP4 code is explicitly for **indexer Q and indexer-compressor KV**, not the 512-wide attention latent: [ds4.c line 3288](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:3288). T-A’s “matching the engine” geometry is therefore attached to the wrong cache object. SAW also uses randomized block-diagonal Hadamard transforms, whereas T-A uses an unrandomized transform.

The analysis additionally discards the Gaussian case post hoc at [line 142](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_fp4_rotation_snr.py:142). That is precisely the disputed distributional assumption. By its own preregistered rule, failure to reach 10 dB killed FP4; the later “not a kill, stack more methods” conclusion is hypothesis generation, not a passed test.

Minimum useful capture: roughly 8,192 rows per layer from eight stratified layers—early, middle, late, and several full-indexer layers—across diverse prompts and positions. That is about 128 MiB for post-baseline-E4M3 non-RoPE latents, or roughly twice that if pre-rounding values are also kept. Capture corresponding queries and indexer selections.

Look first at per-channel RMS/max/kurtosis, block `amax/RMS`, covariance spectrum, whether outlier channels remain stable across prompts and layers, plain versus randomized-BDR FP4 error, query-weighted score error, attention KL, and top-k margin crossings. SnapMLA found tightly concentrated MLA content and outlier-heavy RoPE on a different model; it does not establish GLM-5.2 content-channel outliers. [SnapMLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10718)

### (c) Stacking

**Verdict: not additive. A GLM-5.2 combined estimate is unverifiable.**

Rotation and LinC both attack error caused by anisotropic/outlier directions. Boundary-layer protection removes the most sensitive subset of that same error. KVSink protects a handful of unusually influential rows, again overlapping the sensitive-tail problem. DSA tiering is a storage policy, not an error-reduction method.

KVLinC’s own isolation table demonstrates the overlap. In NLL units, its combined rotation+LinC improvement is only about 64–65% of the sum of their isolated improvements on both reported Llama models. [KVLinC ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05373) UltraQuant provides no isolated value for first/last-layer protection—all its accuracy results already include it, and it still reports benchmark-dependent regressions. [UltraQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20474) KVSink generally protects only five sink tokens and is not a correction for global latent error. [KVSink](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04257)

Therefore the honest planning estimate is:

- Credited result: **2.74 dB on synthetic, unvalidated data**.
- Credit for the proposed additions: **0 dB until measured jointly on GLM-5.2**.
- Combined GLM-5.2 result: **unverifiable**, not 4.8 dB.

SAW-INT4’s near-BF16 GLM-4.7 benchmark results make FP4 plausibility nonzero, but they do not imply your `0.01 nat/token` gate will pass. [GLM-4.7 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19157v1)

### (d) DSA cold rows

**Verdict: the indexer reads every visible row. The “99.8% unread” argument is false—but there is an important cache separation.**

At full-indexer layers, CUDA scoring receives `visible = pos + 1` and the entire separate `layer_indexer_key_cache`: [ds4.c line 46030](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:46030). Top-k happens only afterward at [line 46040](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:46040). The CPU reference makes the all-row loop explicit at [line 12842](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:12842).

However, the scorer reads the separate 128-wide indexer-key cache, not the 512+64 attention cache. Selected attention then reads only chosen rows from `layer_kv_lora_cache` and `layer_k_rope_cache` at [line 46103](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:46103).

Consequences:

- Quantizing only attention cKV does not directly corrupt same-layer top-k selection.
- Quantizing the indexer cache does: every row is scored, and small errors around the 2048th/2049th margin can flip membership.
- A “cold” cKV row may be cold precisely until it becomes semantically important. Its first retrieval still receives FP4 error; selection frequency is not equivalent to importance.
- Earlier attention errors alter later hidden states and indexer queries, so selection can still change indirectly.
- At 1M, current F32 indexer scoring reads roughly `21 × 1M × 128 × 4 = 10.75 GB` per generated token before attention. DSA attention compute is sparse, but indexer work remains O(context). Decode speed at 1M is therefore unverified.

Tiering remains an engineering possibility, but “nearly free fidelity because rows are unread” is dead.

### (e) Honest bottom line

**Verdict: no—do not promise 1M/no-noticeable-loss. State that it is unverified and does not fit with the current 67 GiB expert cache.**

The memory model contains concrete errors:

- T0 assumes 79 cache layers and adds eight nonexistent scales to today’s F32 row at [lines 22–25](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_kv_budget.py:22). The source has 78 normal layers at [ds4.c line 34426](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:34426), and current allocations contain separate cKV, RoPE and indexer arrays but no scale array at [line 39197](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:39197).
- Correct decomposition is approximately `175.5 KiB cKV + 10.5 KiB indexer + 5.34 KiB misc = 191.34 KiB/token`.
- Correct strict-FP8 slope is about **76.77 KiB/token**, not 72.84. The claimed ~168K ceiling therefore becomes approximately **159K**, conditional on the same questionable envelope.
- “99 comparable runs” is not established: the filter merely checks that `main.log` contains the text `binary_sha12`; it does not compare the SHA or serving arguments at [T0 line 68](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_kv_budget.py:68). The fit also uses only the first and last context points at [line 103](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_kv_budget.py:103).

Corrected 1M FP4+F16-RoPE context storage is approximately:

- **48.2 GiB** with an optimistic one-byte exponent per 32-value group.
- **51.7 GiB** with F32 group scales.

Thus `FP4 context + 67 GiB experts + 6 GiB margin = 121.2–124.7 GiB`, already beyond 119.7 GiB before other model/runtime residency. The stated 45.2 GiB omitted FP4 scales and used the wrong layer decomposition.

The codec evidence also stops short:

- T1’s “full image” covers only exponents −40…40 at [line 135](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_fp8_kv_codec.c:135), tolerates signed-zero mismatch, and its source check can silently skip at [line 200](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_fp8_kv_codec.c:200).
- T2 explicitly tests standalone prototype kernels “in isolation” at [line 1](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/harness/test_fp8_kv_kernel.cu:1). The `-0.0` issue was a prototype codec bit-identity bug with zero arithmetic effect, not demonstrated engine fidelity damage.
- Neither test exercises FP4, engine wiring, real cache consumers, or T3.
- The supplied GLM path calls the opaque GPU compact-store function at [ds4.c line 45963](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-bmarti44-spark-deepseek-v4-flash/3109d29d-cd62-4998-a2e1-bfb6e29288b5/scratchpad/engine-src/ds4.c:45963); it does not visibly call the copied DeepSeek-V4 E4M3 CPU quantizer. “GLM is already E4M3-rounded” is therefore **unverifiable from the supplied source**, and T1/T2 do not prove it.

The honest ceilings are:

- **Proven today:** configured 32K; functional retrieval demonstrated only at 11,648 tokens [measurement](/home/bmarti44/spark-deepseek-v4-flash/results/glm52-gates/loadprof-2026-07-25.json:153).
- **Reasonable next engineering target:** 128K using integrated strict FP8+F32-RoPE storage; corrected memory-only ceiling is roughly 150–160K.
- **FP4 ceiling with fidelity:** unverifiable. There is no passed FP4 NLL gate.

Trading expert cache is necessary for 1M and can be rational only when capacity matters more than decode speed. After real runtime residency, the cache would likely have to fall into roughly the low-40-GiB range, but the exact number and resulting tok/s are **unverifiable** without a target-build memory/speed sweep. Do not make that trade until FP4 passes fidelity; otherwise you sacrifice decode speed for a context mode that may still fail quality.
